What is happening

1 · Residuals

For every point, the residual is the vertical gap between the observed y and the value the curve predicts: eᵢ = yᵢ − ŷᵢ.

2 · Least squares

The best curve minimises SSE = Σ eᵢ². Squaring makes all errors positive and penalises big misses more than small ones.

3 · Normal equations

Setting the derivative of SSE to zero gives a linear system (XᵀX)a = Xᵀy. Solving it gives the coefficients directly.

4 · Linearising

Exponential, log and power models are fitted by transforming the data (e.g. taking ln y) so the problem becomes a straight-line fit.

5 · Goodness of fit

R² = 1 − SSE/SST tells you the share of variation in y the model explains. 1 is a perfect fit, 0 is no better than the mean.

6 · Overfitting

A high-degree polynomial can pass through every point (R² → 1) but wiggles wildly between them. Simpler models often predict better.
